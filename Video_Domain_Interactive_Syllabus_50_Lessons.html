01LEVEL 1VIDEO EDITING FOUNDATION5 lessons+

Goal: Understand how video works, set projects up correctly, and complete a first clean edit.

011. How Video Works & the Editing Pipeline⌄

Skills: Frame, frame rate (24, 25, 30, 50/60), resolution, aspect ratio, codec versus container, bitrate, variable frame rate on phone footage. The pipeline: ingest → assembly → rough cut → fine cut → picture lock → audio → color → graphics → export. Vocabulary: raw footage, timeline, sequence, A-roll, B-roll.

Find a video: YouTube search: video frame rate, resolution, codec and bitrate explained

Task: Footage detective. Inspect 6 clips from the pack that have different specs (MediaInfo or Premiere’s Properties). Fill in a spec sheet for each: resolution, fps, codec, bitrate, audio sample rate, duration. Flag which clips will cause trouble in a 1080p, 25 fps sequence and explain why.

Submit: Spec sheet and a one-paragraph summary of the pipeline in your own words.

Pass check: At least 5 of 6 clips correctly described; at least 2 real conflicts identified.

022. Workspace, Shortcuts & Efficiency⌄

Skills: Project panel, Source and Program Monitors, Timeline, Effect Controls, Essential Graphics, Essential Sound, Lumetri. Saving a custom workspace. J-K-L playback, I/O marking, insert and overwrite, ripple delete, add edit, zoom, snapping. Keyboard-first editing habits.

Learning video: Edit Faster With These Hotkeys in Premiere Pro (Adobe Video × Film Riot)

Task: Save a custom workspace under your name. Screen-record a timed assembly of 10 clips into a sequence in under 6 minutes, using keyboard shortcuts for playback, marking and placing clips.

Submit: Workspace screenshot with every panel labeled, the screen recording, and a list of the 15 shortcuts you now use.

Pass check: All major panels labeled correctly; 10 clips placed in order; at least 10 shortcuts visibly used.

033. Project Setup, Ingest & Sequence Settings⌄

Skills: New project, scratch disks, autosave, importing, sequence from clip versus custom settings, matching frame rate to footage, 1080p versus 4K timelines, proxy workflow, checking clip Properties.

Learning video: Adobe Premiere Pro Tutorial: Sequence Settings and Export Settings

Task: Create a project called FirstEdit_Name. Import at least 10 video clips (including one 4K or high-fps clip), 2 audio files and 3 images. Create two sequences: 1920×1080 at 25 fps and 1080×1920 at 25 fps. Create proxies for the heavy clip and show that proxy toggling works.

Submit: Project file, screenshot of Sequence Settings for each sequence, screenshot of the proxy toggle.

Pass check: Sequence settings correct; no offline media; proxy plays smoothly.

044. File Management, Naming & Backup⌄

Skills: Folder structure on disk (Footage, Audio, Graphics, Project, Exports), bins inside the project, naming conventions (date_project_shot), labels, relinking, collecting project files, the 3-2-1 backup rule.

Learning video: Organize Premiere Pro Projects Like a Professional

Task: Build the folder structure and matching bins (Video, Audio, Images, Graphics, Sequences). Rename all assets with one consistent convention. Move the whole folder to another drive, break the links on purpose, then relink. Back it up to a second location.

Submit: Folder-tree screenshot, organized project, and a short note on your naming convention and backup location.

Pass check: Zero offline media after the move; a classmate can find any asset within 30 seconds using only your naming scheme.

055. Shot Language & Your First Edit⌄

Skills: Shot sizes (wide, medium, close-up, insert), camera angles and movement, composition (rule of thirds, headroom, lead room), how focal length and exposure change the image. Shot logging, selects, markers. Basic export: H.264, 1080p, AAC audio.

Learning video: Ultimate Guide to Camera Shots: Every Shot Size Explained

Task: Shoot 20 shots of one simple subject (making tea, a walk across campus) covering all shot sizes. Log each shot (size, usable yes/no, note). Choose 8 selects and edit a logical 30-second sequence. Export an MP4.

Submit: Shot log and a 30-second MP4 (±2 s).

Pass check: At least 4 shot sizes used; no black or flash frames; consistent audio level; the export plays correctly on a phone.

02LEVEL 2CUTTING & TIMELINE CONTROL5 lessons+

Goal: Develop strong editing fundamentals and learn to create clean sequences.

016. Timeline Fundamentals⌄

Skills: Video and audio tracks, track targeting, playhead, snapping, insert versus overwrite, lift versus extract, sync lock, linking and unlinking clips, timeline zoom, nesting basics.

Learning video: Premiere Pro Tutorial for Beginners: Complete Guide (timeline and trimming from 6:33)

Task: Build a gapless 45-second sequence from 8 clips following a supplied shot list. Use insert at least 3 times, overwrite at least 3 times, and ripple delete to close gaps. Nest one group of shots and explain why.

Submit: 45-second MP4, project, and a screen recording that shows the timeline.

Pass check: Duration 45 s ±1; zero gaps; audio and video in sync throughout.

027. Cutting & Splitting⌄

Skills: Deciding when to cut (action, dialogue, reaction, new information), cutting on movement, razor and Add Edit, removing dead air and repetition, keeping information while cutting length.

Learning video: 5 Ways to Cut & Trim Clips in Adobe Premiere Pro

Task: Cut 3 minutes of raw interview or event footage down to 45 seconds. A supplied checklist names 5 key facts that must remain. Remove false starts, long pauses and repetition.

Submit: Raw clip, final cut, and a 5-line list of what you removed and why.

Pass check: All 5 key facts kept; no distracting jump cuts; final length 45 s ±2.

038. Trimming Techniques⌄

Skills: Ripple, roll, slip and slide edits, Trim Mode, extend edit, frame-accurate trimming with the keyboard, spotting flash frames and late or early cuts.

Learning videos: Ripple and Rolling Editing in Premiere Pro · Slip and Slide Editing in Premiere Pro

Task: Fix a supplied rough cut with 12 deliberate flaws (gaps, flash frames, late cuts, early starts, one sync problem), using the right trim tool for each. Then build your own 30–45 second montage where every shot starts and ends at an intentional moment (action peak, look, beat).

Submit: Fixed sequence, a list of the 12 flaws with the tool used for each, and the montage.

Pass check: At least 11 of 12 flaws fixed; ripple, roll, slip and slide each used at least once.

049. Continuity, Match Cuts & Pacing⌄

Skills: 180° rule, eyelines, screen direction, continuity of action, match cuts, when jump cuts work, cutaways, average shot length, montage, the Kuleshov effect.

Learning videos: What is Continuity Editing? · Don’t Cross the Line: the 180 Degree Rule

Task: Edit a simple 30-second action scene (entering a room, sitting down, opening a laptop) from several angles with correct continuity. Then re-cut the same footage at a very different tempo (calm versus energetic). Write 3 lines on how pacing changed the feeling.

Submit: Both versions and the 3-line note.

Pass check: No continuity or screen-direction errors; average shot length reported for both versions and clearly different.

0510. Basic Editing Workflow (Campus Video)⌄

Skills: The full small-project workflow: ingest, log, selects, assembly, rough cut, fine cut, review, revision, export. Giving and receiving feedback. Versioning (v1, v2).

Learning video: Premiere Pro Tutorial for Beginners: Full Course in 1 Hour

Task: Edit a 60-second campus-life video with a clear opening, middle and ending, plus music with fades. Export v1, collect written feedback from 2 peers on a supplied form, make at least 3 changes and export v2.

Submit: v1, v2, feedback forms, change log, and the project folder organized as in Lesson 4.

Pass check: Clear three-part structure; length 60 s ±3; v2 visibly addresses at least 3 notes.

Pass check: Each effect is justified; the image still looks natural; no leftover unused effects.

0515. Graphics Templates & Still Images⌄

Skills: Reusable Motion Graphics Templates (.mogrt), brand kits (colors, fonts, logo), importing logos and stills (transparent PNG, resolution), pan and zoom on photos, scaling without losing quality.

Submit: Brand-kit sheet, .mogrt file, final video.

Pass check: Template editable with new text; photos sharp at full-screen view; branding consistent.

04LEVEL 4AUDIO & SOUND DESIGN5 lessons+

Goal: Make the video sound as good as it looks.

0116. Understanding Audio⌄

Skills: Dialogue, music, ambience and effects as separate layers. Reading waveforms, decibels, peaking and clipping, 48 kHz sample rate for video, mono versus stereo, microphone types (lavalier, shotgun, phone), room tone, clap sync, track organization.

Learning video: Premiere Pro Tutorial for Beginners: Complete Guide (audio and music from 20:00)

Task: Take a 30-second scene and organize its audio onto labeled tracks: Dialogue, Music, SFX, Ambience. In a supplied problem clip, find and mark three faults (clipping, hum, uneven level) with markers.

Submit: Project, MP4, and a screenshot of the labeled tracks and markers.

Pass check: Track layout consistent; all 3 faults found.

0217. Levels, Mixing & Loudness⌄

Skills: Gain versus clip volume, fades, ducking music under speech, Essential Sound panel, meters. Targets: speech peaking roughly between −12 and −6 dB, true peak below −1 dB, about −14 LUFS for online delivery and −23 LUFS for EBU R128 broadcast. Checking on headphones and on a phone speaker.

Learning video: Controlling LUFS (loudness) in Adobe Premiere Pro

Task: Mix a 45-second talking-head clip with background music. Duck the music roughly 10 dB under speech and let it rise in pauses. Measure integrated loudness and land at −14 LUFS ±1.

Submit: 45-second mixed video, a loudness-meter screenshot, and a note on the phone-speaker check.

Pass check: Loudness in range; no clipping; a fresh listener understands every word on a phone speaker.

0318. Noise Reduction & Dialogue Cleanup⌄

Skills: Noise types (hiss, hum, room echo, wind, traffic), high-pass EQ, noise reduction, de-reverb, de-essing, compression, Enhance Speech and its limits, room-tone fills, removing clicks and breaths, avoiding watery or robotic artifacts.

Learning video: How Aidin Robbins Uses Premiere Pro to Clean Up His Audio

Task: Clean a noisy 30-second voice recording (supplied) using a layered approach (edit, EQ, noise reduction, compression) and lay it over B-roll. Make a second version with an AI tool such as Enhance Speech and compare the two.

Submit: Before/after audio, final video, and a table of settings used and artifacts you hear.

Pass check: Clearer speech without robotic or watery sound; notes name at least 2 artifacts or limits.

0419. Sound Effects, Foley & Audio Keyframes⌄

Skills: Whooshes, hits, risers, ambience beds, foley (footsteps, cloth, props), layering, syncing to picture, pitch and EQ variation, volume keyframes and automation, panning for movement, sourcing and licensing sounds.

Find a video: YouTube search: sound design and foley for video editing

Task: Create a 30-second product or action edit with at least 8 purposeful sound layers: 5 or more effects, one ambience bed, one foley sound you record yourself, and music with keyframed fades.

Submit: MP4 and an SFX cue sheet (timecode, sound, purpose, source and licence).

Pass check: Each effect lands within 2 frames of its action; every sound licensed or self-recorded; cue sheet complete.

0520. Music Editing, Voiceover & Licensing⌄

Skills: Choosing music for mood and tempo, editing a track to length (cutting on bars, crossfades, loop points), voiceover technique (quiet room, mic distance, pop control), syncing voiceover to B-roll, copyright (royalty-free, licensed, Creative Commons attribution, Content ID claims).

Find a video: YouTube search: edit music to fit video length in Premiere Pro

Task: Record a 60-second voiceover for a short explainer, clean it and cut it over B-roll. Re-edit a supplied 2:30 music track to exactly 60 seconds with a natural ending. Keep a licence log of every asset.

Submit: 60-second explainer, raw voiceover, licence log.

Pass check: Music edits unnoticeable; ends on a musical resolution; every asset in the log has a source and terms.

05LEVEL 5MOTION & SPEED5 lessons+

Submit: Final MP4, a 3-way comparison clip, and a short note.

Pass check: High-fps slow motion is smooth with no warping; the comparison correctly names the artifacts.

0323. Speed Ramping⌄

0424. Stabilization & Reframing⌄

0525. Introduction to After Effects & Motion Graphics⌄
